# Meridian Technologies — Strategic Plan 2023–2026

*Approved by the Board of Directors, October 2022. Authored under the
leadership of CEO Lenore Pak. **Status: largely superseded.** Retained for
historical context.*

---

## Executive Summary

Meridian's three-year plan (2023–2026) commits the company to a transition
from a self-serve, SMB-led collaboration tool toward a multi-segment platform
with a center of gravity in mid-market and enterprise. Five strategic pillars
guide the plan.

## Pillar 1 — Move Up Market

**Ambition:** Grow enterprise ARR from $74M (Q4 2022) to $250M (Q4 2026).
**Tactics:**
- Build a dedicated enterprise sales team (target: 80 quota-carrying reps by
  end of 2024).
- Stand up a customer success function for accounts >$100K ACV.
- Develop industry-specific compliance certifications (FedRAMP Moderate,
  HIPAA, GxP).
- Build a partnership with a top-tier global SI by mid-2024.

**Status (Q1 2025 retrospective):** Partially executed. Enterprise reps grew
to ~75 by end of 2024. FedRAMP Moderate achieved Q3 2024. HIPAA achieved
Q1 2024. GxP not yet pursued. Top-tier SI partnership signed with a Tier 2
firm, not a Tier 1. **Enterprise ARR Q4 2024: $138M** — well below the
implied trajectory.

## Pillar 2 — Modernize the Platform

**Ambition:** Refactor core PM platform onto a microservices architecture by
end of 2024 to enable faster feature shipping.
**Status:** Refactor began Q1 2023, originally scheduled for completion Q4
2024, now estimated Q3 2025. Roughly six months behind. Engineering team
attributes the delay to scope creep and to dependencies on AI Copilot
infrastructure that didn't exist in the original plan.

## Pillar 3 — Operational Efficiency

**Ambition:** Expand operating margin from 4% (2022) to 18% (2026) by
disciplined GTM spend and back-office automation.
**Status:** Margin expanded to 10.5% (Q4 2024) — on plan. Achieved primarily
through reduced SMB acquisition spend and a 2024 G&A reorganization.

## Pillar 4 — Selective International Expansion

**Ambition:** Build out direct presence in EMEA (London, Berlin, Amsterdam)
and APAC (Singapore, Sydney) by end of 2025. Target 18% of revenue from
international by 2026.
**Status:** EMEA executed (London Q2 2023, Berlin Q4 2023, Amsterdam Q3 2024).
APAC not yet stood up. International contribution Q4 2024: 12% of revenue.

## Pillar 5 — Selective M&A

**Ambition:** Pursue one to two acquisitions per year, focused on adjacent
collaboration capabilities (e.g., resource management, time tracking,
workflow automation).
**Status:** Zero acquisitions in 2023 or 2024. Three deals reviewed and
declined for valuation reasons. The plan did not anticipate AI-native
acquisition opportunities.

## Implicit Bets That Did Not Survive Contact

A retrospective candor: this plan made several implicit bets that turned
out to be wrong, or that the market overtook before we could execute.

1. **Speed of agentic AI adoption.** The plan, written in late 2022, treated
   generative AI as a feature to add — not as a category-defining shift in
   how knowledge work is done. By mid-2024 it was clear that agentic AI was
   reshaping the entire collaboration software category.

2. **SMB as a controlled wind-down.** The plan deliberately reduced SMB
   acquisition spend, on the theory that SMB customers would naturally
   migrate to mid-market over time. In practice, SMB customers churned
   faster than projected — to cheaper competitors and AI-native upstarts
   — and very few migrated up.

3. **Build-versus-buy for AI.** The plan presumed Meridian could build
   competitive AI features in-house on a modest R&D increase. In practice,
   the engineering required was significantly larger and Meridian was
   18+ months behind the leading AI labs in talent density.

4. **Mid-market as an automatic engine.** The plan treated mid-market as a
   resilient growth engine. In practice, mid-market net retention compressed
   (115% to 102%) as customers became more price-sensitive and as competitors
   bundled AI features into mid-tier plans.

## Notes for the Incoming CEO

This plan was the right plan for late 2022. It is no longer the right plan
for 2025. Three pillars (move up market, operational efficiency, international
expansion) have produced real progress and should not be abandoned. Two
pillars (platform modernization, selective M&A) need to be substantially
rethought in light of agentic AI.

The board has authorized the new CEO to write a successor plan and to bring
it back for approval no later than Q1 2026.
