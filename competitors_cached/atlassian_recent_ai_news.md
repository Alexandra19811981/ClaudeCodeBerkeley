# Atlassian — AI Posture Summary (cached)

*Cached snapshot, ~February 2026, for fallback use if WebFetch fails.*

## Headline positioning

Atlassian publicly positions itself as **"the agentic enterprise platform
for software and IT teams"** (recent Atlassian blog posts; Co-CEO commentary
on the most recent earnings call). Their agent offering is branded **"Rovo"**.

Atlassian has been the most explicit of the four competitors about an
agentic positioning. Their internal language is "Atlassian is becoming an
agent company that ships software."

## Most recent flagship announcement

In **late January 2026**, Atlassian announced **"Rovo Studio"** — a
developer-targeted agent builder that runs natively on Atlassian
infrastructure (Jira, Confluence, Bitbucket). Rovo Studio is sold as a
**separate paid product**, distinct from existing Atlassian licenses.

This is a notable contrast to Asana and Monday, which bundle. Atlassian
has bet that enterprise IT/dev teams will pay for a premium agent
offering separately.

## Pricing posture

- Existing Atlassian products (Jira, Confluence, etc.): unchanged
  per-seat pricing.
- **Rovo:** Per-seat + per-action consumption hybrid. Per-seat starts at
  ~$20/seat/month. Consumption charged per "Rovo action" above a baseline.
- This is the **only major PM/collaboration competitor with consumption
  pricing today.**

## Strategic positioning

Atlassian is the most committed to **"agentic platform"** positioning of
the four. Their messaging has shifted decisively away from "tools for
teams" toward "the platform on which teams' agents operate." Their CEO
has said publicly that he expects Atlassian's agent revenue to exceed
its traditional product revenue by 2028.

Atlassian's differentiator vs. agent-native upstarts is **the existing
install base** in enterprise IT and developer organizations. Atlassian
already has Jira and Confluence in nearly every Fortune 500 software org;
Rovo is built to deepen that footprint.

## Likely place vs. Meridian's options

Atlassian is **the closest direct comparable to Meridian's Option B**
(agentic work platform with enterprise governance). If Meridian picks
Option B, Meridian will be positioned alongside Atlassian — and the
question for investors will be "why Meridian and not Atlassian?" The
answer probably has to be **vertical depth (regulated industries Atlassian
doesn't serve well: pharma, banking) and PM rigor (Atlassian's Jira is
software-developer-shaped, not general-PM-shaped).**

If Meridian picks Option A, Atlassian will look like the more forward-leaning
agent platform and Meridian will look like the cautious traditional player.

## Open questions

- Atlassian has been quiet about specific model partnerships, but their
  Rovo materials reference Anthropic by name in two places (most other
  competitors do not name a model lab).
- Atlassian's enterprise governance story is strong on the dev side but
  thinner outside engineering organizations.
- Atlassian made a small acquisition of an agent observability startup
  in October 2025 (purchase price undisclosed), suggesting an interest
  in evaluation/safety tooling.
