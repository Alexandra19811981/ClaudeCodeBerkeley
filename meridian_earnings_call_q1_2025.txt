OPERATOR
  Good afternoon and welcome to Meridian Technologies' first quarter 2025
  earnings call. At this time, all participants are in a listen-only mode.
  After the speakers' presentation, there will be a question and answer
  session. I will now turn the call over to Tara Linwood, Head of Investor
  Relations.

TARA LINWOOD
  Thank you, operator, and good afternoon. Joining me today are Catherine Park,
  our newly appointed President and CEO, and David Hoyt, our CFO.

  Before we begin, I'd like to remind everyone that today's discussion will
  contain forward-looking statements. With that, I'll turn it over to Catherine.

CATHERINE PARK
  Thank you Tara, and good afternoon everyone. Before I cover the quarter,
  let me say it's a privilege to be here. I joined Meridian thirty days ago,
  and I want to thank Lenore for the foundation she leaves us — three
  thousand five hundred customers, a strong enterprise franchise, and one
  of the best engineering teams in collaboration software.

  My focus over the next ninety days is to listen, to meet our largest
  customers and partners, and to be honest with this board, this team, and
  with you about where we are.

  Let me give you the headline numbers and then I want to spend most of my
  time talking about what I'm seeing.

  Q1 revenue was ninety-six point four million dollars, up twelve percent
  year over year. ARR ended the quarter at three hundred ninety-nine million,
  also up about twelve percent. Operating margin expanded to eleven percent,
  free cash flow margin sixteen percent.

  These are decent results in absolute terms. They are the slowest growth
  rates this company has reported as a public company.

  I want to be direct. The deceleration we are seeing — twenty-eight percent
  growth in 2022, nineteen percent in 2023, sixteen percent in 2024, and
  on track for low double digits in 2025 — is not transitory. It is
  structural. We have to address it.

  Three things I am seeing.

  First, our enterprise business is performing well. Net revenue retention
  in enterprise is one hundred twenty-five percent. We are winning displacement
  deals, particularly in financial services and life sciences. This is a real
  asset. We will invest behind it.

  Second, our SMB business has deteriorated faster than I think this team
  fully appreciated coming in. Net retention in SMB is below ninety percent
  and gross logo churn is north of twenty percent annualized. The previous
  strategic plan deliberately deprioritized SMB. I think the deprioritization
  is correct in direction. The execution has been chaotic.

  Third, on AI. We launched our AI Copilot to limited beta in Q4 of last
  year. Adoption is — to put it kindly — early. Meanwhile, every category
  competitor and several well-funded startups are out in the market with
  agentic AI features that customers are asking us about on every call.
  We are behind, and the gap is widening.

  In ninety days I will come back to you with a strategic plan. Until then,
  no major changes to guidance, no big M&A announcements. Thirty days of
  listening, sixty days of planning. With that, David.

DAVID HOYT
  Thanks Catherine. Q1 revenue ninety-six point four, up twelve percent.
  Subscription revenue ninety-one point one. Services revenue five point
  three. Gross margin seventy-nine point four percent. Sales and marketing
  expense as a percentage of revenue thirty-three point four percent, down
  from thirty-five point two percent in the year-ago quarter as we continue
  to be disciplined on go-to-market spend.

  R&D expense was twenty-four point nine percent of revenue, up modestly
  as we increase investment in our AI roadmap. G&A ten point oh percent.

  Operating margin eleven point one percent. Free cash flow fifteen point
  five million, sixteen percent margin.

  Cash and equivalents four hundred thirty-eight million at quarter end.
  No debt. We repurchased thirty million of stock during the quarter under
  our existing authorization.

  Looking ahead, we are reiterating our 2025 revenue guidance of three
  hundred ninety to four hundred five million, implying eight to twelve
  percent growth. Operating margin guidance of twelve to thirteen percent.

  With that, operator, please open the line for questions.

OPERATOR
  Our first question comes from Allison Park at Morgan Stanley.

ALLISON PARK
  Hi. Catherine, welcome. A question on the AI Copilot rollout. You mentioned
  adoption is early. Can you put some metrics around it? And how should we
  think about the timeline for general availability?

CATHERINE PARK
  Allison, thanks. Honest answer — paying seats on AI Copilot at the end of
  Q1 are zero. The product is in a closed beta with about forty enterprise
  customers. We are still resolving real product issues in beta. I expect
  GA in Q3 at the earliest. I'd rather be late and right than on time and
  embarrassed.

ALLISON PARK
  Got it. And follow-up — does the previous AI roadmap survive your review?

CATHERINE PARK
  Parts of it. I'd say more than half of what's on the roadmap I would
  question. We'll come back to you in ninety days.

OPERATOR
  Next question, Brent Costa, Goldman Sachs.

BRENT COSTA
  Catherine, on SMB. You said it's deteriorated faster than the team
  appreciated. Can you quantify? And is there a scenario where you simply
  exit SMB?

CATHERINE PARK
  Brent, on the numbers — SMB was about eighteen percent of ARR as of
  last quarter, down from thirty-eight percent at the end of 2022. Net
  retention in SMB is in the mid eighties. On the question of exiting —
  nothing is off the table, but I want to be careful. SMB historically
  is the top of the funnel that brings logos into mid-market and
  eventually enterprise. If we cut SMB to zero, do we starve the funnel?
  I don't yet know the answer. We'll have one in ninety days.

OPERATOR
  Priya Raman, JPMorgan.

PRIYA RAMAN
  Hi. Capital allocation question. You have four hundred thirty-eight
  million in cash, no debt. Buyback or M&A?

CATHERINE PARK
  Priya, both are on the table. Frankly, with our valuation where it is
  today, the buyback math is attractive. On M&A, I am specifically looking
  at AI-native companies that could accelerate our roadmap by twelve to
  eighteen months. I would rather buy capability than rebuild it.

OPERATOR
  Karim Ouedraogo, Citi.

KARIM OUEDRAOGO
  Catherine, can you talk about competitive intensity? Specifically with
  Asana and Monday on the AI side.

CATHERINE PARK
  Asana shipped their AI agent feature in the fall and they are hammering
  it in every deal we are in. Monday has been more aggressive on price
  with AI features bundled in their mid-tier plans. We are losing some
  deals on AI feature parity. Not most deals — but enough that I am
  paying attention.

OPERATOR
  Wendy Bao, Wells Fargo.

WENDY BAO
  Hi. Cultural question. You're new. What's the team's mindset right now?

CATHERINE PARK
  Honestly, mixed. Engineering is frustrated about roadmap thrash on AI.
  Sales is frustrated about losing deals on features the roadmap promised
  but hasn't delivered. Customer success is energized — our enterprise
  customers love us. My job in the next ninety days is to give the
  organization a clear story and stop the thrash.

OPERATOR

CATHERINE PARK
  Thank you. Three things to remember from today. The deceleration is real
