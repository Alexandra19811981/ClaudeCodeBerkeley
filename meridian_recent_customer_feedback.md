# Customer Feedback — Last 90 Days

*Synthesized by the Customer Insights team from: enterprise advisory board
sessions, mid-market quarterly business reviews, sales win-loss interviews,
and inbound product feedback. Date range: Dec 2025 – Feb 2026.*

---

## Top themes

### 1. Enterprise customers want governance for AI agents (urgent)

Mentioned in 18 of 22 enterprise advisory board sessions. Specific asks:

- **Auditability.** "I need to know which agent took which action on which
  data, by which user, at what time. Today I cannot answer that question
  for any AI tool we run."
  — VP IT, top-5 US bank
- **Role-based agent permissions.** "Our analysts can run an agent.
  Compliance officers cannot. The agent itself needs different access for
  different users running it."
  — CIO, global pharma
- **Model selection / model bring-your-own.** "Our security team has
  approved Claude. They have not approved every model. Let us pin which
  model your agent uses."
  — Head of AI governance, financial services regulator
- **Data residency.** "Agents must process EU customer data only in EU
  regions. This is non-negotiable."
  — Multiple EMEA enterprise customers

This is a category in which Meridian is well-positioned to win because of
existing governance infrastructure. Most of our PM competitors have not
built this and would take 12–18 months to catch up. AI-native upstarts
have not built this either and may never (it's expensive, slow, regulated
work).

### 2. Mid-market customers want AI features bundled, not unbundled

Mentioned in roughly 60% of mid-market QBRs. The dominant message:

- "We are not paying $40 a seat extra for Copilot. Asana includes their
  agent in standard tier. So does Monday. We don't see why we should be
  charged for it."
- "We renew at our current ARR or we leave."

Pricing pressure is real and accelerating. Holding Copilot at $40/seat is
defensible for enterprise; less defensible for mid-market.

### 3. SMB is bleeding to AI-native upstarts faster than to PM competitors

Win-loss interviews from Q4 2025 SMB churn:

- 41% lost to ClearAI Work, Linear AI, or other AI-native tools
- 28% lost to Asana / Monday / ClickUp on price
- 18% downgraded usage rather than fully churning
- 13% other (closed business, M&A, etc.)

The AI-native upstarts are smaller in absolute terms but their churn-attraction
to our SMB base is meaningful and growing.

### 4. The "what is Meridian" question is showing up in win-loss

In 14 of 31 lost mid-market deals in Q4 2025, the buyer cited some version
of:

- "We weren't sure if you were a PM tool that does AI or an AI tool that
  does PM."
- "Your sales team and your marketing told us different stories."

This is a positioning problem, not a feature problem.

### 5. Customers do not want to be locked into a single LLM provider

Mentioned unprompted in 11 of 22 enterprise advisory sessions. Customers
are deeply skeptical of AI tools that hard-code one model. They want
optionality — Claude today, GPT tomorrow, an internal model for sensitive
workloads, and the ability to switch when prices change.

This is an argument *against* a tight strategic partnership with any single
model lab and *for* a "neutral" enterprise positioning.

## What customers are not saying

- **Almost no one is asking for a "marketplace of agents" feature** in
  the next 12 months. Mostly mentioned in the engineering / DevOps
  user persona, not the buying persona.
- **Almost no one is asking for fully autonomous workflows.** Even the
  most enthusiastic enterprise customers want human-in-the-loop checkpoints
  for high-stakes actions.

## Recommendations to the CEO

1. **Lead with governance as the differentiator.** Whatever positioning
   you pick, make governance the through-line. It's the moat customers
   actually want.
2. **Reconsider mid-market Copilot pricing.** Either bundle it into the
   standard tier or differentiate the value clearly. The current pricing
   is a renewal headwind.
3. **Be model-neutral publicly.** Do not tie Meridian's positioning to
   any one model lab. Customers will use that against us.
4. **The SMB story has to acknowledge AI-native displacement directly.**
   Pretending it's still a price-sensitivity story is no longer credible.
